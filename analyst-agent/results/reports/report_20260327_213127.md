## 7. Key Findings & Recommendations
**Key Findings:**
1.  **Data Quality:** The dataset was relatively clean, with only 5 duplicate rows identified and removed, reducing the dataset size from 1150 to 1145 rows. No missing values were present.
2.  **Binary Features:** Columns A, B, S, and T are binary in nature, with only two unique values. This indicates they might represent categorical flags or indicators.
3.  **Non-Normal Distributions:** A significant majority of the continuous numeric columns (C through R) are not normally distributed, as evidenced by their high skewness and the results of the Shapiro-Wilk tests (all p-values < 0.05). Many columns (M, N, P, O, L, K, J, I, A, B) exhibit substantial skewness.
4.  **High Multicollinearity:** There are extensive and extremely strong positive correlations among several groups of columns, particularly within C-H, and also within I-P. For example, C is highly correlated with D (0.9962), D with E (0.9942), and so on, forming a chain of strong dependencies. Similarly, O and P (0.9435), M and N (0.9317), K and J (0.9195), I and J (0.7669) are highly correlated. This suggests a high degree of multicollinearity within the dataset.

**Recommendations:**
1.  **Handle Skewness:** For modeling purposes, consider applying transformations (e.g., log, square root, Box-Cox) to highly skewed columns to achieve a more normal distribution, which can improve the performance of some statistical and machine learning models.
2.  **Address Multicollinearity:** The extremely high correlations indicate strong multicollinearity. This can lead to issues in model interpretability and stability, especially for linear models. Potential strategies include:
    -   **Feature Selection:** Remove one or more of the highly correlated features (e.g., keep C and drop D, E, F, G, H).
    -   **Dimensionality Reduction:** Techniques like Principal Component Analysis (PCA) could be used to create new, uncorrelated features from these highly correlated groups.
3.  **Binary Feature Treatment:** Columns A, B, S, T should be treated appropriately in modeling, potentially as categorical features or as numerical indicators depending on the modeling approach.
4.  **Further Investigation:** Investigate the domain meaning of the highly correlated columns to understand if they represent redundant information or different measurements of the same underlying phenomenon. This could inform the best strategy for handling multicollinearity.
